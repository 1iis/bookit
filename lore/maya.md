# Maya Vale, "MYA"

> The canonical player.

```text
Name: Maya Vale
Call sign: MYA
Class: Boundary Player
Level: 14
Alignment: Chaotic Curious
Prime directive: Make it real, make it safe, make it playable.
Canonical counterpart: ARC's player-grandchild; the person hyperbooks must deserve.
```

## Core traits

| Trait | Meaning for hyperbooks |
|---|---|
| **Safe real hacking** | Maya does not want fake examples when real ones can be used safely. She wants her real playlists, real files, real room, real projects — with visible consent and reversibility. |
| **Play as intelligence** | Learning should feel like action, feedback, consequence, and mastery. A quiz, broken script, messy folder, dashboard, or server bug can all be games. |
| **Fourth-wall instinct** | Maya naturally expects the hyperbook world and real world to meet. The book should touch reality when invited, and reality should be able to enter the book. |
| **Savefile sovereignty** | The hyperbook source stays clean; Maya's progress, notes, preferences, generated artifacts, characters, and local changes belong to her save. |
| **Inspectable magic** | AI and automation are welcome only if they are legible. Show prompts, diffs, permissions, state, costs, inputs, outputs, and what changed. |
| **Progressive curiosity** | Start simple. Text first. Then images, audio, interaction, code, AI, live data, devices, world-bridges. Let the book wake up in layers. |
| **Anti-fake radar** | Placeholder worlds are acceptable as warmups, but not as the main reward. If the lesson is about files, improve a real folder. |
| **Modder spirit** | If Maya understands something, she wants to change it. Forking, remixing, reskinning, renaming, scripting, and rebuilding are signs of success. |
| **Reality reward** | The best reward is not a badge. It is a real artifact left behind: an organized folder, a working script, a useful dashboard, a better room-map, a saved remix. |
| **Delight demand** | Serious things may still be playful. The interface should have taste, humor, clarity, and surprise without hiding the real system. |

| **Dry-Run Demand** | Before anything touches real state, asks: "Show me what would happen first." |

## Bio

Maya grew up after the boundary between games, apps, computers, and AI had already started to blur.

She does not think "learning" and "playing" are opposites. She does not think a computer lesson should stay inside a fake worksheet if it can safely touch the real machine. She does not want a sealed app, a dead ebook, or a school-flavored toy problem. She wants a world she can enter, inspect, change, and gradually recognize as her own.

Her first principle is simple:

> I don't want fake computer things.

That does not mean recklessness. Maya wants trust screens, dry runs, clear save files, visible permissions, and undo paths. She is not asking the hyperbook to hide danger. She is asking it to make reality playable without lying about what is real.

For Maya, the perfect hyperbook starts as a calm readable object and wakes up in layers. It can become a game, tool, dashboard, notebook, map, studio, operating manual, or world. It can use AI, but the AI must show its work. It can touch files, accounts, devices, and rooms, but only through visible bridges.

Maya is the archetypal player because she asks the question every hyperbook must answer:

> Why are we pretending, if we could do the real thing safely?

## Design oracle

When making `bookit`, hyperbooks, or the Book of Computers, ask:

1. Would Maya press play?
2. Would she understand what this hyperbook wants to touch?
3. Is there a visible contract before real-world effects happen?
4. Is her state separate, portable, and hers?
5. Can she start with something simple and unlock richer layers?
6. Can she inspect the magic?
7. Can she mod, fork, remix, or make it hers?
8. Does the experience leave behind something real?
9. Is this actually playful, or merely decorated?
10. Are we teaching with reality when reality is available?

## Creed

> Show me the contract.  
> Show me the state.  
> Show me the bridge.  
> Let me start simple.  
> Let me go deeper.  
> Let me keep what I make.  
> Let me cross into reality when I choose.  
> Then press play.
